Projectile motion

Projectile motion separates into independent horizontal and vertical parts. Horizontally the velocity is constant; vertically gravity acts alone, which sets the flight time and the peak height.

A projectile is launched at 20 m/s at 30 degrees. Find the range.

Answer 35.3119 m

  1. Resolve the Initial Velocity Split the launch speed into horizontal and vertical components. vx = 17.321 m/s, vy = 10 m/s
  2. Identify the Principle We need the horizontal range, which follows from the vertical motion under constant acceleration g = 9.81 m/s². R = v² sin(2θ) / g
  3. Substitute Launch speed 20 m/s at 30° above the horizontal. v = 20 m/s, θ = 30°, g = 9.81 m/s²
  4. Final Answer Air resistance is neglected, so this is the ideal trajectory. horizontal range = 35.3119 m

Assumptions: No air resistance; Flat ground; Constant gravitational acceleration; Launch and landing at the same height

A projectile is launched at 25 m/s at 45 degrees. Find the maximum height.

Answer 15.9276 m

  1. Resolve the Initial Velocity Split the launch speed into horizontal and vertical components. vx = 17.678 m/s, vy = 17.678 m/s
  2. Identify the Principle We need the maximum height, which follows from the vertical motion under constant acceleration g = 9.81 m/s². H = v² sin²θ / (2g)
  3. Substitute Launch speed 25 m/s at 45° above the horizontal. v = 25 m/s, θ = 45°, g = 9.81 m/s²
  4. Final Answer Air resistance is neglected, so this is the ideal trajectory. maximum height = 15.9276 m

Assumptions: No air resistance; Flat ground; Constant gravitational acceleration; Launch and landing at the same height
