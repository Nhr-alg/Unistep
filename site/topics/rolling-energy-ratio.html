Rolling energy ratios

A body rolling without slipping shares its kinetic energy between translation and rotation. The split depends only on the shape, because the moment of inertia fixes the ratio I/(MR²).

A solid sphere rolls without slipping. Find the ratio of rotational KE to total KE.

Answer 2/7

  1. The Core Strategy To compare the energies we need one common variable. Because the solid sphere rolls without slipping, the linear velocity and angular velocity are linked. v = ωR
  2. Defining the Shape Factor Each shape has its own mass distribution, captured by the moment of inertia. For a solid sphere, I = (2/5)MR². The factor 2/5 is the key constant here. I = (2/5)MR²
  3. Deriving Rotational Energy Substitute the moment of inertia and ω = v/R into the rotational kinetic energy formula. The radius cancels, so the ratio does not depend on the size of the object. Kr = 1/2 Iω² = 1/2 * (2/5 MR²) * (v/R)² = 1/5 Mv²
  4. Translational Contribution The centre of mass moves like a particle, which gives the standard translational kinetic energy. Kt = 1/2 Mv²
  5. Final Synthesis Total energy is the sum of both contributions. Total K = 1/2 Mv² + 1/5 Mv² = 7/10 Mv². The ratio Kr / Total K is therefore (1/5) / (7/10). Ratio = 2/7

Assumptions: Pure rolling (no slipping); Uniform density; Rigid body

A thin ring rolls without slipping. Find the ratio of rotational KE to total KE.

Answer 1/2

  2. Defining the Shape Factor Each shape has its own mass distribution, captured by the moment of inertia. For a thin ring, I = MR². The factor 1 is the key constant here. I = MR²

  5. Final Synthesis Total energy is the sum of both contributions. Total K = 1/2 Mv² + 1/2 Mv² = 1 Mv². The ratio Kr / Total K is therefore (1/2) / (1). Ratio = 1/2
