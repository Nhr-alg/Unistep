Ohm's law

Ohm's law relates voltage across a component to the current through it and its resistance. Any one of the three can be found from the other two.

A voltage of 12 V across a resistance of 4 ohm. Find the current.

Answer 3 A

  1. Identify the Principle We need the current, and the known quantities point to I = V / R. I = V / R
  2. Substitute the Values Use base SI units so the result comes out in the correct unit without extra conversion. I = 12 / 4 = 3 A
  3. Final Answer Check the magnitude: a small resistance with a large voltage should give a large current. current = 3 A

Assumptions: Ohmic component (constant resistance); Steady direct current; Negligible internal resistance

A current of 2 A through a resistance of 5 ohm. Find the voltage.

Answer 10 V

  1. Identify the Principle We need the voltage, and the known quantities point to V = I · R. V = I · R
  2. Substitute the Values Use base SI units so the result comes out in the correct unit without extra conversion. V = 2 × 5 = 10 V
  3. Final Answer Check the magnitude: a small resistance with a large voltage should give a large current. voltage = 10 V

Assumptions: Ohmic component (constant resistance); Steady direct current; Negligible internal resistance
