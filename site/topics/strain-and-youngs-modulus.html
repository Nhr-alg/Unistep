Strain and Young's modulus

Young's modulus is the slope of the elastic part of the stress–strain curve. It links how hard a material is pushed to how much it stretches, independently of the component's size.

Find the strain for an elongation of 2 mm over a length of 2 m

Answer 0.001

  1. Convert to Consistent Units Both lengths must be in the same unit before dividing. ΔL = 0.002 m, L = 2 m
  2. Apply the Definition Strain is the change in length divided by the original length. ε = ΔL / L = 0.002 / 2
  3. Final Answer Strain is dimensionless; it is often quoted in microstrain (×10⁻⁶). ε = 0.001 (1000 µε)

Assumptions: Uniform deformation; Small strain

Find Young's modulus for a force of 10000 N on an area of 0.0001 m2 with elongation 0.5 mm over a length of 1 m

Answer 200 GPa

  1. Compute the Stress Stress is the internal force carried per unit of cross-sectional area. σ = F/A = 10000 / 0.0001 = 100 MPa
  2. Compute the Strain Strain is the change in length divided by the original length, so it is dimensionless. ε = ΔL/L = 0.0005 / 1 = 0.0005
  3. Apply the Definition Young's modulus is stress divided by strain in the linear-elastic region. E = σ/ε = 200000 MPa
  4. Final Answer A modulus near 200 GPa is typical of steel. E = 200 GPa

Assumptions: Linear-elastic behaviour; Uniform cross-section; Small deformations
