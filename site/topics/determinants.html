Determinants

The determinant measures how a matrix scales area or volume. A zero determinant means the transformation collapses space onto a lower dimension, which is why singular matrices cannot be inverted.

Find the determinant of [[1,2],[3,4]]

Answer -2

  1. Read the Matrix This is a 2×2 matrix, so its determinant is a single number. A = [[1, 2], [3, 4]]
  2. Expand Along the First Row Multiply each first-row entry by the determinant of the minor left after deleting its row and column, alternating signs. det = ad − bc
  3. Evaluate Substitute the entries and carry out the arithmetic. det = (1)(4) − (2)(3) = -2
  4. Final Answer A non-zero determinant means the matrix is invertible. det(A) = -2

Assumptions: Square matrix; Entries read row by row

Find the determinant of [[2,3,1],[4,1,2],[3,5,1]]

Answer 5

  1. Read the Matrix This is a 3×3 matrix, so its determinant is a single number. A = [[2, 3, 1], [4, 1, 2], [3, 5, 1]]
  2. Expand Along the First Row Multiply each first-row entry by the determinant of the minor left after deleting its row and column, alternating signs. det = a(ei − fh) − b(di − fg) + c(dh − eg)
  3. Evaluate Substitute the entries and carry out the arithmetic. det = 2(1·1 − 2·5) − 3(4·1 − 2·3) + 1(4·5 − 1·3) = 5
  4. Final Answer A non-zero determinant means the matrix is invertible. det(A) = 5

Assumptions: Square matrix; Entries read row by row
